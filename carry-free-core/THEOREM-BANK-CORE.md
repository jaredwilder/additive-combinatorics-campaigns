## CF-01 — Bounded-residue no-carry zero detection

Let `r=(r_0,...,r_{m-1})∈Z^m`, with `|r_j|≤A`, and define

\[
\Phi_B(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} r_j B^j.
\]

For `m≥2`, the implication

\[
\Phi_B(r)=0 \implies r=0
\]

holds for every such `r` **iff**

\[
B>A.
\]

**Proof core.** At the least nonzero coordinate `t`, divisibility by `B` forces `B|r_t`. If `0<|r_t|<B`, impossible. Sharpness for `B≤A` is witnessed by `(-B,1,0,...)`.

## CF-02 — Sharp balanced-cube injectivity

`\Phi_B` is injective on

\[
[-A,A]^m\cap\mathbb Z^m
\]

iff

\[
B>2A.
\]

Hence the least integer base is

\[
2A+1.
\]

**Proof core.** A collision reduces to zero detection for a difference vector in `[-2A,2A]^m`; sharpness again uses the adjacent carry vector.

## CF-03 — Carry-lattice kernel generation

The integer kernel of

\[
\Phi_B:\mathbb Z^m\to\mathbb Z
\]

is generated by the adjacent carry vectors

\[
g_j=B e_j-e_{j+1},\qquad j=0,\dots,m-2.
\]

**Proof core.** Eliminate coordinates from the least index upward: kernel membership forces the current least coordinate to be divisible by `B`.

## CF-04 — Sharp relation localization

Let `a=(a_1,...,a_n)` be integer coefficients and `D` a digit alphabet. Put

\[
M=\max\left\{\left|\sum_\ell a_\ell d_\ell\right|: d_\ell\in D\right\}.
\]

If `B>M`, every global relation

\[
\sum_\ell a_\ell x_\ell=0
\]

between base-`B` digit strings localizes coordinatewise.

This is the standard no-carry lifting mechanism written as an exact threshold statement.

## CF-05 — Integer-root divisibility threshold

For the class of all nonzero integer polynomials whose nonzero coefficients have absolute value at most `C`, positive integer evaluation at `B` is guaranteed nonzero for the entire class **iff**

\[
B>C.
\]

**Proof core.** Factor out the least power of `X`; an integer root `B` must divide the remaining nonzero constant term. Sharpness is `P(X)=X-B`.

## CF-06 — Sparsity does not lower the ordinary positional threshold

Sparsity, degree, or term count cannot lower the universal signed positional base threshold below `2A+1` for alphabet `[-A,A]`, because the sharp obstruction already has support two: the polynomial `X-B`, equivalently `(-B,1)`.

# Full-box extremality

## BOX-01 — Output-span lower bound

If the integer linear scalarization

\[
x\mapsto w\cdot x
\]

is injective on `[-A,A]^m`, then

\[
\|w\|_1\ge \frac{(2A+1)^m-1}{2A}.
\]

**Proof core.** The cube has `(2A+1)^m` points while every output lies in an interval containing at most `2A\|w\|_1+1` integers.

## BOX-02 — Optimality of balanced positional weights

With

\[
q=2A+1,
\]

the weights

\[
1,q,q^2,\dots,q^{m-1}
\]

attain the minimum possible output span and `\ell_1` coefficient norm among integer linear scalarizations injective on the full cube.

## BOX-03 — Rigidity at equality

Equality in the full-box output-span lower bound forces positional weights, up to coordinate permutation and independent sign changes.

**Scope note.** This is a precise canonical-numeration rigidity statement. The original bank marks the result proved internally but flags historical-literature/formalization review as still worthwhile.

## BOX-04 — Heterogeneous mixed-radix extremality

For a rectangular product of digit alphabets with sizes

\[
q_0,\dots,q_{m-1},
\]

mixed-radix weights

\[
1,\ q_0,\ q_0q_1,\ \dots
\]

attain the minimum contiguous output span

\[
\prod_i q_i-1.
\]

The clean uniqueness statement requires a coordinate-order convention compatible with the capacities.

# Abstract algebra / language limits

## ALG-01 — No unbounded additive embedding `Z^m -> Z`

For `m>1`, no additive group homomorphism

\[
\mathbb Z^m\to\mathbb Z
\]

is injective.

Every such map is `x↦w·x` and has a nontrivial kernel.

## ALG-02 — Net balance cannot characterize prefix constraints

No decision rule depending only on terminal additive totals can characterize a language whose validity depends on prefix order. The two words `(+1,-1)` and `(-1,+1)` have the same terminal total but can have different prefix validity.

# Sparse reconstruction

## SP-01 — Sparse moment uniqueness

An integer vector with support at most `2s` is determined by its first `2s` power moments

\[
\sum_i z_i i^r,\qquad r=0,\dots,2s-1.
\]

**Proof core.** Restrict to the nonzero support and invert the corresponding Vandermonde matrix.

## SP-02 — Sparse carry-free moment encoding

The weights

\[
w_i=\sum_{r=0}^{2s-1} i^r B^r
\]

with

\[
B>4Asm^{2s-1}
\]

produce an injective integer-linear scalar encoding of all `s`-sparse vectors in `[-A,A]^m`.

**Proof core.** No-carry decoding recovers the first `2s` moments of the difference; SP-01 forces equality.

## SP-03 — Finite-field sparse syndrome injectivity

Let

\[
p>\max(m,2A)
\]

be prime and let `\alpha_i∈\mathbb F_p` be distinct. The first `2s` Vandermonde syndromes

\[
\sum_i x_i\alpha_i^r,\qquad r=0,\dots,2s-1,
\]

uniquely determine every `s`-sparse integer vector

\[
x\in[-A,A]^m.
\]

A collision gives a `2s`-sparse field vector in a square Vandermonde nullspace; `p>2A` makes coefficient lifting unique.

## SP-04 — Corrected `2s` measurement lower bound

At least `2s` linear field measurements are necessary to recover **every** `s`-sparse vector over the whole field.

This does **not** automatically prove `2s` optimality for a restricted bounded integer alphabet embedded in the field.

**Proof core.** If `r<2s`, an `r×m` matrix has a dependent set of at most `r+1≤2s` columns, yielding a nonzero sparse kernel vector over the field.

# Classical coding-theory consequence

## ROB-02 — Optimal linear channel redundancy

A `k`-symbol linear message over a field needs at least `2t` additional symbols to correct `t` arbitrary symbol errors, and at least `t` additional symbols to detect `t` errors or correct `t` known erasures. MDS codes attain the corresponding Singleton-bound limits when the required parameters exist.

# Binary sparse additive encoding

## BS-01 — Counting lower bound

If positive weights bounded by `W` uniquely encode every subset of `[m]` of size at most `s` by its sum, then

\[
sW+1\ge\sum_{r=0}^{s}\binom mr.
\]

Therefore, for fixed `s`,

\[
W=\Omega_s(m^s).
\]

## BS-02 — Shifted `B_s` encoding for variable cardinality

Given a cyclic `B_s` set `a_i mod N`, the shifted weights

\[
w_i=L+a_i,
\qquad L=s(N-1)+1,
\]

uniquely encode every subset of size at most `s` by its integer sum.

The large shift first forces equal cardinalities; reduction modulo `N`, with equal padding, invokes the `B_s` property.

## BS-03 — Optimal binary sparse exponent

For fixed `s`, the minimum possible largest positive weight needed for exact additive encoding of all subsets of `[m]` of size at most `s` satisfies

\[
W(m,s)=\Theta_s(m^s).
\]

This combines BS-01 with classical cyclic `B_s` constructions and BS-02.

# Signed support-two encoding

## SGN-01 — Counting lower bound

Any positive-weight scalar encoding of every

\[
x\in\{-1,0,1\}^m
\]

with support at most two requires

\[
\max_i w_i\ge \frac{m^2}{2}.
\]

There are `2m^2+1` legal states and at most `4W+1` integer sums in `[-2W,2W]`.

## SGN-02 — Shifted Sidon encoding

If `{a_i}` is a Sidon set in `[0,N]` and

\[
L>4N,
\]

then

\[
w_i=L+a_i
\]

uniquely encode all signed support-two states in `{-1,0,1}^m`.

The shift separates the total signed charge; Sidon sums and differences resolve the remaining collisions.

## SGN-03 — Optimal signed support-two exponent

For positive weights exactly encoding every signed support-two state,

\[
W(m,2)=\Theta(m^2).
\]

The lower bound is SGN-01 and the upper bound follows from SGN-02 plus classical `O(m^2)` Sidon constructions.
